## Choose where the name adds value

A [leather folio](/#gift-leather-folio) can carry an approved name in a discreet position for everyday business use. For an [original precision-metal piece](/#gift-cnc-sculpture), consider a dedicated inscription area as part of the design. The available method and letter detail depend on the selected surface and finish; approve them on a sample.

For a recognised technology product, start with a named card or presentation sleeve. Keep the device’s model information, controls and original packaging legible. For a [MUSE architectural oud burner](/#gift-oud-majlis), explore the name on the presentation case or card before deciding whether the object itself should be personalised. This keeps the inscription separate from heat-handling components.

## Use the occasion to choose the message

For Eid employee appreciation, a shared Arabic–English greeting can sit alongside each colleague’s approved name. For Eid Al Etihad, use the campaign’s approved message and distinguish the recipient’s name from the company identity. For a client milestone or service award, confirm the title and recognition wording with the person responsible for the relationship.

A household gift needs a different addressee from an individual professional gift. Ask whether the card is addressed to a person, a family or a business host. Use our [UAE gifting calendar](/guides/uae-corporate-gifting-calendar/) to choose the occasion, then work backwards from the actual distribution date. Set separate cutoffs for names, artwork and packed-set approval within the agreed project schedule.

## One recipient row, two approved name fields

Ask the buyer’s designated contact to supply and approve both language versions. Do not create an Arabic spelling by guessing from an English name, or silently shorten a long name to fit. An approved short form, a larger inscription area or a card-only name are choices to return to the buyer.

Keep the recipient list in a controlled project file. A reference such as R001 links the row to the item and packing check; it is an internal matching aid, not a replacement for the recipient’s name on the gift. Collect the information needed for the gift and agreed delivery, without adding unrelated personal details.

- Recipient reference; exact Arabic name; exact English name; approved title if used.
- Gift model or variant; name location; greeting version; destination or distribution group.
- Artwork version; approval status; approver; any exception still awaiting a decision.

## Review the exported proof, not only the name list

Place Arabic and English in a deliberate layout, with room for the longest approved name. Check the exported artwork at production size: spelling, letter connections, line breaks, alignment, title placement and the relationship between name and logo. Have the designated Arabic reviewer approve the Arabic version as displayed.

Mixed right-to-left and left-to-right text can place punctuation or numbers unexpectedly. Review names, English job titles, dates and reference numbers in the actual exported proof, not just in the spreadsheet that supplied them. Resolve any layout change before releasing the artwork.

For engraving, foil or debossing, the physical sample answers a separate question: are the letters readable on this material, at this size and depth? Review contrast, detail and placement on the selected surface. An approved digital proof alone does not approve the physical finish.

## Match the finished item, card and box before sealing

Consider a campaign with two gift variants. The name may be correct on each card while a card is placed in the wrong box. Use the same recipient reference through the packing list, named item and card check; keep any internal matching label out of the gift’s presentation.

Before sealing, check the recipient reference, approved name, selected model, accessories, card and presentation variant together. Record the completed check against that row. A missing name, conflicting spelling or changed gift choice goes into an exception list for resolution before its set is released.

- Keep the approved name list and artwork version together; avoid parallel unlabelled copies.
- Separate confirmed recipients from late additions awaiting artwork or sample decisions.
- If a name or title changes, check every affected item, card and packing record before release.
